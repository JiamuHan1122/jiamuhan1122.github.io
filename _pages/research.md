My research interests lie in **video understanding and computer vision**. I am particularly interested in how models represent temporal structure, identify meaningful action instances, and reason about human motion and interactions.

## Temporal Action Detection

Temporal action detection aims to localize action instances in untrimmed videos, including their start and end times. I am interested in temporal boundary modeling, multi-scale context, and sequence architectures for capturing long-range dependencies. An important question in my current work is when distant temporal context genuinely contributes useful evidence to action localization.

## Action Recognition and Video Understanding

I study spatiotemporal representations that capture appearance, motion, and the evolution of actions over time. My broader goal is to move beyond coarse action labels toward a more detailed understanding of what happens in a video and how it unfolds.

## Fine-grained 3D Human Interaction Understanding

I explore how 3D skeletons, body-part kinematics, spatial relations, and motion trajectories can reveal differences between interactions that may share similar high-level labels. This direction also considers how occlusion, viewpoint, and 3D estimation errors affect the evidence available to a model.

## Multimodal Learning with Missing Modalities

I am also interested in robust multimodal learning when one or more modalities are missing or noisy. In multimodal sentiment analysis, I study how local affective dynamics, modality reliability, and evidence insufficiency can influence prediction sensitivity and decision drift.
